Borrelia

Antimicrobial Discovery CenterNortheastern University · Wellesley College Biochemistry honors thesis (2019)

Borrelia burgdorferi and Lyme disease, including evaluating anecdotal Lyme disease treatments in search of borreliacidal compounds.

My Biochemistry honors thesis at Wellesley College, conducted with the Antimicrobial Discovery Center at Northeastern University, made three contributions to the study of Borrelia: (1) identifying several Borrelia-inhibitory compounds, including a growth accelerator and a Borrelia killer several times more effective than the control antibiotic; (2) drawing novel synthetic connections from existing data relevant to the ecological mitigation and clinical treatment of Lyme disease; and (3) designing and piloting a novel semi-lawn plating approach to address a primary lack of lawn-type testing in Borrelia research. Related earlier work at the Center includes an internship on Borrelia-inhibitory compounds (2018) and an REU in the Lewis Lab testing microbial metabolites for antimicrobial action (summer 2017).
